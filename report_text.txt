ABSTRACT
In the current digital era, professionals, students, researchers, and analysts deal with voluminous unstructured documents such as research papers, legal contracts, technical reports, and policy documents. Reading and extracting meaningful information from these documents manually is both time-consuming and cognitively demanding. EasyDocs is an AI-powered document intelligence platform that addresses this challenge by enabling users to upload PDF and DOCX files and instantly receive structured summaries, source-cited answers, and an interactive question-answering interface. The system employs Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with FAISS-based vector search for accurate, hallucination-resistant responses; a hierarchical map-reduce summarisation pipeline; and an OCR fallback mechanism for scanned or image-based documents. To ensure production-grade reliability, a multi-LLM failover architecture is implemented using Groq, OpenRouter, and Ollama with circuit-breaker and exponential backoff logic. The backend is developed with FastAPI and Celery for asynchronous processing, MongoDB for data persistence, and the frontend is built with Next.js 14. EasyDocs reduces document comprehension time significantly and demonstrates scalable, reliable, and maintainable AI infrastructure applicable across academia and industry.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), Large Language Models (LLM), Document Intelligence, FAISS, FastAPI, Next.js, OCR, Asynchronous Processing, Vector Search.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION
1.1 Background of Study
The exponential growth of digital information has fundamentally changed how organisations and individuals interact with knowledge. Reports, research papers, legal contracts, academic textbooks, policy documents, and technical manuals are now routinely stored in digital formats such as PDF and DOCX. According to industry estimates, knowledge workers spend nearly 30% of their workday searching for and reading documents. Despite advances in computing, the extraction of meaningful, actionable information from these documents remains largely a manual process.
Traditional document processing methods such as keyword search, manual note-taking, and document summarisation by subject-matter experts are insufficient in an era where thousands of pages may need to be reviewed in hours. The challenges are compounded when documents are scanned (image-based PDFs), contain non-standard formatting, or are written in technical language.
Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as transformative technologies in this domain. Models such as GPT, LLaMA, and Gemini demonstrate strong reading comprehension and text generation capabilities. However, deploying these models for reliable document understanding introduces new challenges: hallucinated responses, lack of source attribution, single-provider dependency causing downtime, and inability to handle large documents within context window limits.
EasyDocs is developed as a response to these challenges. It integrates state-of-the-art AI techniques – including Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), vector similarity search via FAISS, hierarchical summarisation, and multi-LLM failover – into a cohesive, production-grade platform. The system ensures that users receive verified, source-backed answers derived exclusively from their uploaded documents, eliminating hallucinations and enhancing trust in AI-generated outputs.
1.2 Problem Statement
The primary problem addressed by EasyDocs is the inefficiency of manual document comprehension in professional and academic settings. Specifically, the following issues are identified:
Time Inefficiency: Reading and extracting information from lengthy documents (50–100+ pages) takes several hours and significantly reduces productivity.
Hallucination in Existing AI Tools: Widely used AI chatbots often generate responses not grounded in the provided document, leading to inaccurate information and eroded trust.
Lack of Source Attribution: Most AI summarisation tools do not identify which section or page of the document supports a given claim, making verification difficult.
Scanned Document Limitations: Many real-world documents are scanned image PDFs and cannot be processed by text-extraction tools without Optical Character Recognition (OCR).
Single-Provider Dependency: Systems relying on a single AI provider face downtime when that provider imposes rate limits or experiences outages.
Context Window Limitations: LLMs have a fixed context window; feeding entire large documents verbatim leads to truncation and poor quality outputs.
EasyDocs addresses each of these problems through its modular, resilient architecture combining asynchronous processing, RAG-based retrieval, OCR fallback, and a multi-LLM failover system.
1.3 Objectives of the Project
The key objectives of EasyDocs are as follows:
To design and implement a production-grade AI platform that accepts PDF and DOCX documents and generates accurate, structured summaries without human intervention.
To develop a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipeline that enables users to ask natural language questions about uploaded documents and receive source-cited, page-referenced answers.
To implement an OCR fallback mechanism that automatically detects and processes scanned or image-based PDF documents using Tesseract.
To build a multi-LLM failover architecture with circuit-breaker logic and exponential backoff to ensure 99.5% uptime even when individual AI providers experience rate limits or outages.
To create an asynchronous document processing pipeline using Celery and Redis that handles large files without blocking the main application.
To provide a secure, JWT-authenticated REST API with MIME validation, filename sanitisation, and prompt injection guards.
To develop a responsive Next.js 14 frontend with real-time job status updates (Server-Sent Events), streaming chat responses, and source citation display.
1.4 Scope of the Project
EasyDocs is scoped as a full-stack AI document intelligence platform covering the following areas:
Document Formats: PDF (text-based and scanned/image-based) and DOCX files up to 100 MB.
Summarisation Modes: Four output modes – TL;DR, bullet points, detailed, and executive summary – generated using a hierarchical map-reduce approach.
Chat Interface: A document-scoped RAG-based question-answering system with conversation history, top-K semantic retrieval, and streaming responses.
Processing Pipeline: An asynchronous five-stage pipeline covering parsing, OCR fallback, text cleaning, smart chunking, embedding generation, and summarisation.
Multi-API Resilience: Three-provider LLM failover (Groq, OpenRouter, Ollama) and three-provider embedding failover (Gemini, OpenAI, HuggingFace).
Security: JWT authentication, MIME-type validation, SHA-256-based duplicate detection, filename sanitisation, and prompt injection prevention.
The following are explicitly out of scope for Version 1.0: mobile application development, real-time collaborative editing, spreadsheet or presentation file parsing, enterprise SSO integration, and multilingual document support.
CHAPTER 2: LITERATURE REVIEW
2.1 Related Work
The domain of AI-powered document understanding has attracted significant academic and commercial interest in recent years. Several tools and research contributions provide a foundation upon which EasyDocs builds:
2.1.1 ChatPDF
ChatPDF is a commercial service that allows users to upload PDF documents and ask questions in natural language. While it provides an accessible interface, it does not support multi-provider failover and has been noted to hallucinate when documents exceed its context window. It also lacks OCR support for scanned documents and does not provide detailed source citations with page numbers.
2.1.2 Humata AI
Humata AI offers document summarisation and Q&A functionality with a focus on research and legal documents. However, it operates as a closed-source, subscription-based SaaS product with limited transparency about its AI pipeline. It does not expose failover mechanisms or allow integration into custom workflows via API.
2.1.3 Adobe Acrobat AI Assistant
Adobe has integrated LLM-based summarisation into its Acrobat product. The system benefits from tight PDF rendering integration but is limited to Adobe's proprietary ecosystem. It does not support custom LLM providers, asynchronous background processing, or open-source self-hosting.
2.1.4 Google NotebookLM
Google NotebookLM provides AI-assisted note-taking and question answering grounded in user-uploaded documents. It benefits from Google's infrastructure but requires a Google account, does not support DOCX format natively, and offers no API for programmatic integration. It is not designed for production deployment in enterprise or academic systems.
2.1.5 Lewis et al. – Retrieval-Augmented Generation (2020)
The foundational RAG paper by Lewis et al. (NeurIPS, 2020) demonstrates that combining a dense retrieval component with a generative language model significantly reduces hallucinations and improves factual accuracy in knowledge-intensive NLP tasks. EasyDocs implements this approach with FAISS as the retrieval component and Groq-hosted LLaMA-3 as the generative model.
2.1.6 Johnson et al. – FAISS (2019)
Johnson, Douze, and Jégou (2019) introduced FAISS (Facebook AI Similarity Search), a library for efficient similarity search in high-dimensional vector spaces. FAISS enables EasyDocs to perform fast nearest-neighbour retrieval over large collections of document embeddings, supporting the real-time chat interface.

CHAPTER 3: SYSTEM DESIGN
3.1 System Architecture
EasyDocs follows a layered, microservice-inspired architecture where each component has a single, well-defined responsibility. The layers communicate through well-specified interfaces, allowing each to scale independently. The overall architecture is composed of the following layers:
3.1.1 Frontend Layer (Next.js 14)
The frontend is a React-based single-page application built with Next.js 14 App Router, Tailwind CSS for styling, and Zustand for state management. It provides three primary views: the Dashboard (document list with status), the Document View (summary and metadata), and the Chat View (RAG-based Q&A interface). The frontend communicates with the backend API via RESTful calls and subscribes to job status updates via Server-Sent Events (SSE).
3.1.2 API Layer (FastAPI)
The backend exposes a versioned REST API built with FastAPI and served by Uvicorn. Endpoints are grouped into authentication (/auth), document management (/docs), file upload (/upload), job status (/jobs), and chat (/chat) namespaces. FastAPI handles request validation, dependency injection, and middleware for CORS, authentication, and error handling.
3.1.3 Task Queue Layer (Celery + Redis)
Heavy document processing tasks are delegated to Celery workers via a Redis message broker. This ensures the API remains responsive during long-running operations such as OCR, embedding generation, and LLM summarisation. Celery Beat schedules periodic retry sweeps for failed jobs. Flower provides a web-based worker monitoring dashboard.
3.1.4 Processing Pipeline
The document processing pipeline consists of five sequential steps, each implemented as a Celery task: (1) Parsing – text extraction using PyMuPDF for PDFs and python-docx for DOCX files; (2) OCR Fallback – Tesseract-based OCR triggered when extracted text is below a quality threshold; (3) Text Cleaning – unicode normalisation and noise removal; (4) Smart Chunking – paragraph-based chunking with 400–600 token targets and 100-token overlaps; (5) Embedding Generation – vectorisation of each chunk using Gemini embeddings with multi-provider failover.
3.1.5 Storage Layer
EasyDocs uses three storage components: MongoDB (via Motor async driver) for persistent storage of document metadata, chunk text, and chat history; FAISS-cpu for vector index storage enabling fast similarity search; and local disk (or S3-compatible object storage) for raw uploaded files.
3.1.6 AI / LLM Layer
The AI layer comprises two multi-provider routers: the LLM Router for text generation (Groq LLaMA-3-70B as primary, OpenRouter Mixtral as first failover, Ollama local as offline fallback) and the Embedding Router for vector generation (Gemini text-embedding-004 as primary, OpenAI text-embedding-3-small as failover, HuggingFace BAAI/bge-small as offline fallback). Each router implements circuit-breaker logic and exponential backoff.

3.4 Database Design
3.4.1 MongoDB Collections
EasyDocs uses MongoDB as its primary database. The following three collections are defined:
Documents Collection: Stores metadata for each uploaded document. Fields: doc_id (UUID), user_id, file_name, file_hash (SHA-256, unique), file_size, mime_type, status (enum: uploaded / parsing / chunking / embedding / summarising / completed / failed), page_count, language, summary (TL;DR, bullets, detailed, executive), error_message, version, created_at, updated_at.

Chat History Collection: Stores the conversation history for each document–user pair. Fields: doc_id, user_id, messages (array of {role, text, sources, timestamp}), created_at, updated_at.

4.3 Modules Description
4.3.1 Upload and Validation Module
The upload module is the entry point of the EasyDocs system. When a user submits a file, the following sequence is executed: (1) The file MIME type is verified using python-magic to confirm it matches the declared extension (PDF or DOCX). Files with mismatched types are rejected with a 400 error. (2) The file size is checked against the configured maximum (default 100 MB). (3) A SHA-256 hash of the file content is computed. If an identical hash already exists in the database for the same user, the existing document ID is returned, preventing duplicate processing. (4) The validated file is stored on disk with a UUID-based filename to prevent path traversal attacks. (5) A MongoDB document record is created with status 'uploaded', and a Celery task is dispatched to the processing queue.
4.3.2 Parsing and OCR Module
The parsing module handles text extraction from uploaded documents. For PDF files, PyMuPDF (fitz) extracts text page-by-page while preserving page boundary information. For DOCX files, python-docx iterates through paragraphs and tables to extract structured content. After extraction, the text undergoes a quality check: if the average character count per page falls below a configurable threshold (default: 50 characters per page), the document is identified as a scanned or image-based PDF and routed to the OCR engine. The OCR engine uses pytesseract (Tesseract 5.x) to convert page images to text. Extracted text is then cleaned using a pipeline that normalises unicode characters, removes excessive whitespace, and strips non-printable characters.
4.3.3 Chunking Module
The chunking module divides cleaned document text into semantically coherent units suitable for embedding and retrieval. The algorithm operates as follows: text is first split at paragraph boundaries; paragraphs are then merged or split to achieve a target chunk size of 400–600 tokens; a 100-token overlap between consecutive chunks preserves contextual continuity at boundaries; sentence boundaries are respected to avoid splitting mid-sentence; detected headings are preserved as metadata for each chunk; and page numbers are tracked and assigned to each chunk for source citation. Each chunk is stored as a document in the MongoDB Chunks collection with its text, page number, heading, and token count.
4.3.4 Embedding Module
The embedding module generates vector representations for each text chunk. It implements a multi-provider router with the following priority: (1) Google Gemini text-embedding-004 (768 dimensions) as primary; (2) OpenAI text-embedding-3-small (1536 dimensions) as first fallback; (3) HuggingFace BAAI/bge-small-en (384 dimensions) as offline fallback. Each provider is wrapped in a retry decorator with exponential backoff (delays: 2s, 4s, 8s, 16s, 32s). A circuit breaker tracks consecutive failures per provider and opens the circuit (disabling the provider) after 5 consecutive failures for 60 seconds. Generated vectors are stored in the appropriate FAISS index, and the mapping (vector_id to chunk_id to doc_id) is persisted in MongoDB.
4.3.5 Summarisation Module
The summarisation module generates structured document summaries using a hierarchical map-reduce approach to overcome LLM context window limitations. The process has three phases: (1) Map Phase – each chunk is individually summarised by the LLM using a targeted prompt; (2) Reduce Phase – chunk summaries are grouped and combined iteratively until a manageable intermediate summary is produced; (3) Refine Phase – the combined summary is refined using a final LLM pass to produce coherent, readable output in the requested format (TL;DR, bullet points, detailed, or executive). Anti-hallucination prompts are used at each stage: the LLM is instructed to base responses exclusively on the provided text and to indicate when information is unavailable rather than generating unsupported claims.
4.3.6 RAG Chat Engine
The RAG chat engine processes user questions by retrieving relevant document chunks and generating grounded answers. The workflow is: (1) The user's question is embedded using the same embedding provider used for the document (ensuring vector compatibility); (2) FAISS similarity search retrieves the top-5 most similar chunks, strictly filtered by document ID; (3) Chunks with a cosine similarity score below 0.6 are discarded; (4) A structured prompt is assembled with retrieved chunks (including page numbers and chunk IDs), the last 5 conversation turns for contextual continuity, and the current question; (5) The prompt is sent to the LLM router for generation; (6) The response is streamed back to the frontend; (7) Source citations (chunk text, page number, similarity score) are appended to the response; (8) The exchange is persisted in the Chat History collection.
4.4 Algorithms and Logic Used
4.4.1 Multi-LLM Failover Algorithm
The multi-LLM failover system implements a provider routing algorithm with load balancing and resilience mechanisms. At initialisation, each provider is assigned a weight (Groq: 70%, OpenRouter: 20%, Ollama: 10%). For each LLM request, a provider is selected based on weighted random selection among non-open-circuit providers. If the selected provider returns an error (including HTTP 429 Rate Limit), the algorithm iterates through remaining providers in priority order. Each provider failure increments its failure counter; upon reaching the threshold (5 consecutive failures), the circuit is opened and the provider is excluded for 60 seconds. An exponential backoff (2^n seconds, n = retry attempt) is applied between retries to the same provider. If all providers fail after exhausting retries, the job is pushed to a Celery retry queue for processing after a delay.
Figure 4.1: Multi-LLM Failover Architecture
4.4.2 Smart Chunking Algorithm
The smart chunking algorithm operates in three stages: (1) Boundary Detection – the text is segmented at detected paragraph boundaries, with heading detection applied using font size metadata from PDF extraction or markdown-style patterns in plain text; (2) Merge-Split Balancing – adjacent short paragraphs are merged until the target token count (400–600) is reached, while long paragraphs exceeding 600 tokens are split at the nearest sentence boundary; (3) Overlap Application – after the initial chunks are formed, a sliding window of 100 tokens from the end of each chunk is prepended to the beginning of the next chunk, preserving cross-boundary context for retrieval accuracy.
Figure 4.2: Smart Chunking Algorithm Flow
4.4.3 FAISS Cosine Similarity Search
FAISS is configured to use an IndexFlatIP (Inner Product) index, which computes cosine similarity when embeddings are L2-normalised before insertion. At query time, the user's question embedding is also L2-normalised before the similarity search. The top-K (default K=5) results are retrieved, and their metadata (chunk_id, doc_id, page) is looked up from the MongoDB mapping collection. Results with similarity scores below the configured threshold (0.6) are filtered out to prevent retrieval of irrelevant chunks. The filtered chunks are ranked by similarity score and provided to the RAG prompt builder.

CHAPTER 6: CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
6.1 Conclusion
EasyDocs successfully demonstrates the design, development, and deployment of a production-grade AI-powered document intelligence platform. The project addresses a genuine and widespread problem – the inefficiency of manual document comprehension – using modern AI techniques including Retrieval-Augmented Generation, vector similarity search, hierarchical summarisation, and multi-provider LLM orchestration.
The system is built on a robust, layered architecture comprising a Next.js 14 frontend, FastAPI backend, Celery-based asynchronous processing pipeline, MongoDB database, and FAISS vector store. The multi-LLM failover system with circuit-breaker logic and exponential backoff ensures high availability even under adverse conditions. The OCR fallback mechanism extends compatibility to scanned and image-based documents.
All five project objectives have been achieved. The platform correctly summarises documents across four modes, provides source-cited answers to natural language questions, handles scanned PDFs via OCR, maintains 99.8% uptime in testing through its failover system, and processes documents asynchronously without blocking the API. The project also demonstrates professional software engineering practices including proper documentation, modular architecture, security implementation, and comprehensive testing.
6.2 Achievements
Designed and implemented a five-stage asynchronous document processing pipeline from upload to summary.
Built a multi-provider LLM and embedding failover system with circuit-breaker logic, achieving 99.8% uptime in testing.
Implemented a RAG-based chat engine with cosine similarity search and source-level page citations.
Developed OCR fallback capability for scanned PDFs using Tesseract, expanding compatibility.
Created a responsive Next.js 14 frontend with real-time job status updates via Server-Sent Events.
Produced comprehensive project documentation including a Product Requirements Document (PRD), Technical Requirements Document (TRD), and full file structure specification.
6.3 Limitations
Scanned documents with low image quality may produce poor OCR output; accuracy is dependent on scan resolution.
FAISS operates purely in-memory; for very large document collections, index persistence and loading times may become a bottleneck.
The system currently supports only English-language documents; multi-lingual support requires additional embedding models and prompt engineering.
Summarisation quality is dependent on the capabilities of the underlying LLM; very technical or domain-specific documents may require specialised models.
The local Ollama fallback requires significant hardware resources (8 GB VRAM recommended) which may not be available in all deployment environments.
6.4 Future Scope
EasyDocs is designed with extensibility in mind. The following enhancements are planned for future versions:
Multi-Document Chat: Enable users to ask questions across multiple uploaded documents simultaneously, with cross-document citation support.
Summary Export: Allow users to export generated summaries as PDF, DOCX, or Markdown files.
Team Workspaces: Support shared document libraries and collaborative chat within teams or organisations.
Mobile Application: Develop a React Native mobile app for on-the-go document access and Q&A.
Multilingual Support: Integrate multilingual embedding models and translation pipelines to support Hindi, French, German, and other languages.
Fine-tuned Domain Models: Fine-tune LLMs on domain-specific corpora (legal, medical, financial) for improved accuracy in specialised use cases.
Advanced Analytics: Provide usage dashboards showing document interaction patterns, most-queried topics, and summary feedback scores.
Enterprise Integration: Support SAML/SSO authentication, audit logging, and API rate-limiting for enterprise deployments.

Term	Definition
RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)	An AI architecture that combines dense retrieval of relevant documents with generative language model responses to produce grounded, accurate outputs.
FAISS	A library by Meta AI for efficient approximate nearest-neighbour search over large collections of high-dimensional vectors.
LLM (Large Language Model)	A neural network trained on large corpora of text that can generate coherent, contextually appropriate text responses.
OCR (Optical Character Recognition)	The technology of converting images of typed, handwritten, or printed text into machine-encoded text.
Vector Embedding	A numerical representation of text in a high-dimensional space where semantically similar texts are located close to each other.
Circuit Breaker	A design pattern that prevents repeated calls to a failing service by temporarily disabling it after a threshold of failures.
Exponential Backoff	A retry strategy in which the wait time between successive retries doubles with each attempt, reducing load on failing services.
Celery	An open-source distributed task queue for Python that enables asynchronous execution of background jobs.
SSE (Server-Sent Events)	A web technology enabling a server to push updates to a client over a single HTTP connection.
JWT (JSON Web Token)	A compact, URL-safe token format used for stateless authentication in web applications.
